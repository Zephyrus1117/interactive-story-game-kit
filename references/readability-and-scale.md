# Readability And Scale

## Scale Gates

Use these gates unless the user gives a different target.

| Mode | Nodes | Chapters | Endings | Single playthrough |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| prototype | 25-40 | 3+ | 3+ | 10-18 min |
| standard | 60-120 | 5-8 | 5-8 | 25-45 min |
| longform | 150-300+ | 8+ | 8-15 | 45-120 min |

Never present a prototype as a finished story. If the story has fewer than 45 nodes, explicitly call it a prototype and make the plot unusually direct.

## Clarity Gate

The first three playable nodes must answer:

- Who is the player?
- Where are they?
- What do they want right now?
- What pressure, danger, promise, or loss makes action necessary?

The first chapter must add:

- the first concrete obstacle
- at least one named relationship or institution
- the first irreversible or remembered choice
- a visible short-term goal

## Anti-Mystification Rules

Avoid these failure modes:

- replacing plot with symbols
- withholding all cause and motive until the ending
- using many proper nouns before the player knows why they matter
- writing choices that sound evocative but do not say what the player does
- making every paragraph a metaphor
- ending on ambiguity before the player understands the conflict

Useful test: after chapter one, the player should be able to summarize the premise in one sentence without guessing.

## Plainness Ratio

For atmospheric genres, balance style with explanation:

- At least 60% of paragraphs should describe concrete events, actions, dialogue, or discoveries.
- At most 40% should be mood, metaphor, sensation, or abstract reflection.
- Put the clearest sentence at the start or end of a node.

## Node Quality Checklist

Each non-ending node should include at least one:

- action taken
- information gained
- relationship changed
- resource gained or lost
- route opened or closed
- risk increased
- visible question answered

If a node only creates mood, merge it with a neighboring node.

## Choice Text Rules

Choice labels should state the action plainly:

- Good: `Ask Mei why she hid the receipt`
- Good: `Break the seal and read the decree`
- Weak: `Trust the silence`
- Weak: `Follow the feeling`

Poetic labels are allowed only when the concrete action is still obvious from context.
